Learn the essentials

What is antibiotic resistance?

Antibiotics are valuable medicines. Understanding when they help—and when they do not—is important for everyone.

01

Antibiotics treat bacteria

They treat some infections caused by bacteria. They do not work against viruses.

02

Resistance is in germs—not people

Bacteria can change over time, so some antibiotics may no longer work well against them.

03

Everyone has a role

Good hygiene, vaccination, and responsible medicine use can reduce infections and resistance.

Myth or fact?

“Antibiotics cure colds and flu.”

Most colds and flu are caused by viruses. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections, unless a clinician identifies a bacterial infection as well.

Everyday actions

Five ways to help

Small, practical choices can protect you, your community, and the effectiveness of antibiotics.

  1. 1

    Use antibiotics only when prescribed

    A qualified healthcare professional should decide whether they are needed.

  2. 2

    Follow professional advice

    Use medicines exactly as instructed. Ask a doctor or pharmacist if you have questions.

  3. 3

    Never share or reuse antibiotics

    Do not take leftovers or give your medicines to someone else.

  4. 4

    Prevent infections

    Wash hands, practise food safety, and keep recommended vaccinations up to date.

  5. 5

    Share reliable information

    Help friends and family learn that antibiotics do not work for viruses.
